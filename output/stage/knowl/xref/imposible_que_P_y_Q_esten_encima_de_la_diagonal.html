Remark 1.7.3.

If \((P,Q)\) is a Jacobian pair, then \(v_{1,-1}(P) > 0\) or \(v_{1,-1}(Q) > 0\text{.}\) In fact, by Proposition 1.4.10, we have
\begin{equation} v_{1,-1}(P) + v_{1,-1}(Q) - (1-1) \ge v_{1,-1}([P,Q]) = 0.\tag{1.7.1} \end{equation}
Assume by contradiction that \(v_{1,-1}(P) \le 0\) and \(v_{1,-1}(Q) \le 0\text{.}\) Then \(v_{1,-1}(P) = v_{1,-1}(Q) = 0\) and (1.7.1) becomes an equality. Hence, on one hand, by Proposition 1.4.10, we have \([\ell_{1,-1}(P), \ell_{1,-1}(Q)] \ne 0\text{;}\) while, on the other hand, from \(v_{1,-1}(P) = v_{1,-1}(Q) = 0\text{,}\) it follows that \(\ell_{1,-1}(P), \ell_{1,-1}(Q) \in K[z]\text{,}\) for \(z = xy\text{,}\) which leads to the contradiction \([\ell_{1,-1}(P), \ell_{1,-1}(Q)] = 0\text{.}\)
