    Section 1.7 The shape of counterexamples and (m,n)-pairs

    We will see that a (hypothetical) counterexample to the plane Jacobian conjecture can be transformed into a standard counterexample (see Definition 1.7.17).
    🔗

    Remark 1.7.1.

    By equality (1.2.2), one can readily verify that if \((P,Q)\) is a Jacobian pair and \(P\) is a monomial, then necessarily \(P \sim x\) or \(P \sim y\text{.}\) Consequently, by Proposition 1.3.10 and version 2 of the Plane Jacobian conjecture, if \((P,Q)\) is a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, then neither \(P\) nor \(Q\) can be a monomial.
    🔗
    🔗
    From now on the previous remark will be used without explicit mention.
    🔗

    Definition 1.7.2.

    Let \(m,n \in \mathbb{N}\) be coprime with \(n,m > 1\text{.}\) A pair \((P,Q)\text{,}\) of elements in \(K[x,y]\text{,}\) is called an \((m,n)\)-pair in \(K[x,y]\) , if
    \begin{equation*} [P,Q] \in K^{\times}, \quad \frac{v_{11}(P)}{v_{11}(Q)} = \frac{v_{10}(P)}{v_{10}(Q)} = \frac{m}{n} \quad\text{and}\quad v_{1,-1}(\operatorname{en}_{10}(P)) < 0. \end{equation*}
    An \((m,n)\)-pair \((P,Q)\) is called a standard \((m,n)\)-pair in \(K[x,y]\) if \(v_{1,-1}(\operatorname{stn}_{10}(P)) < 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Remark 1.7.3.

    If \((P,Q)\) is a Jacobian pair, then \(v_{1,-1}(P) > 0\) or \(v_{1,-1}(Q) > 0\text{.}\) In fact, by Proposition 1.4.12, we have
    \begin{equation} v_{1,-1}(P) + v_{1,-1}(Q) - (1-1) \ge v_{1,-1}([P,Q]) = 0.\tag{1.7.1} \end{equation}
    Assume by contradiction that \(v_{1,-1}(P) \le 0\) and \(v_{1,-1}(Q) \le 0\text{.}\) Then \(v_{1,-1}(P) = v_{1,-1}(Q) = 0\) and (1.7.1) becomes an equality. Hence, on one hand, by Proposition 1.4.12, we have \([\ell_{1,-1}(P), \ell_{1,-1}(Q)] \ne 0\text{;}\) while, on the other hand, from \(v_{1,-1}(P) = v_{1,-1}(Q) = 0\text{,}\) it follows that \(\ell_{1,-1}(P), \ell_{1,-1}(Q) \in K[z]\text{,}\) for \(z = xy\text{,}\) which leads to the contradiction \([\ell_{1,-1}(P), \ell_{1,-1}(Q)] = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Remark 1.7.4.

    Note that if \(\rho \ge -\sigma > 0\text{,}\) and \(i,j \in \mathbb{N}_0\) with \(i > j\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}(i,j) = \rho i + \sigma j > \rho i + \sigma i = (\rho + \sigma) i \ge \rho + \sigma \ge 0. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 1.7.5.

    Assume the Jacobian conjecture is false and let \((P,Q)\) be a counterexample to it. If \(v_{11}(P) \mid v_{11}(Q)\) or \(v_{11}(Q) \mid v_{11}(P)\text{,}\) then there is a counterexample \((\tilde{P}, \tilde{Q})\) with
    \begin{equation*} \gcd(v_{11}(\tilde{P}), v_{11}(\tilde{Q})) < \gcd(v_{11}(P), v_{11}(Q)). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Assume for example that \(v_{11}(P) \mid v_{11}(Q)\) and write \(v_{11}(Q) = k v_{11}(P)\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.3.10, we know that \(v_{11}(P) + v_{11}(Q) \ge 3\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation*} v_{11}(P) + v_{11}(Q) - v_{11}(1,1) \ge 3 - 2 > 0 = v_{11}([P,Q]). \end{equation*}
    Then, by Proposition 1.4.12, we have \([\ell_{11}(P), \ell_{11}(Q)] = 0\text{.}\) Since, moreover \(v_{11}(P) > 0\text{,}\) by Proposition 1.5.1(2b), there exist \(m,n \in \mathbb{N}\) and a \((1,1)\)-homogeneous polynomial \(R\text{,}\) such that
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{11}(P) \sim R^m \qquad\text{and}\qquad \ell_{11}(Q) \sim R^n. \end{equation*}
    Hence \(n = k m\) and \(\ell_{11}(Q) = \lambda \ell_{11}(P^k)\text{,}\) for some \(\lambda \in K^{\times}\text{.}\) It follows that \(Q_1 := Q - \lambda P^k\) satisfies the inequality \(v_{11}(Q_1) < v_{11}(Q)\text{.}\) Moreover, it is clear that \([P, Q_1] = [P,Q] \sim 1\) and \(K[P, Q_1] = K[P,Q]\text{.}\) Hence, by version 3 of the Jacobian conjecture, \((P, Q_1)\) is also a counterexample. Now, we can construct successively \(Q_2, Q_3, \dots\text{,}\) such that \((P, Q_j)\) are counterexamples and \(v_{11}(Q_j) < v_{11}(Q_{j-1})\text{,}\) until \(v_{11}(P)\) does not divide \(v_{11}(Q_j)\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \gcd(v_{11}(P), v_{11}(Q_j)) < v_{11}(P) = \gcd(v_{11}(P), v_{11}(Q)), \end{equation*}
    and so \(\tilde{Q} := Q_j\) and \(\tilde{P} = P\) satisfy the required conditions. When \(v_{11}(Q) \mid v_{11}(P)\) a similar argument gives the proof.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 1.7.6.

    A counterexample \((P,Q)\text{,}\) without divisibility relations of degrees or simply a counterexample \((P,Q)\text{,}\) without divisibility , is a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, such that \(v_{11}(P) \nmid v_{11}(Q)\) and \(v_{11}(Q) \nmid v_{11}(P)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Lemma 1.7.7.

    Assume that the Jacobian conjecture is false, let \((P,Q)\) be a counterexample to it and let \((\rho,\sigma) \in [(1,-1),(0,1)] \subseteq \mathfrak{V}\text{.}\) Assume that there exist coprime \(m,n \in \mathbb{N}\text{,}\) such that
    \begin{equation} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(P).\tag{1.7.2} \end{equation}
    Then
    🔗
    1. \(v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} v_{\rho\sigma}(P)\text{,}\)
      🔗
    2. \(v_{\rho\sigma}(P), v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) > 0\text{,}\)
      🔗
    3. \([\ell_{\rho\sigma}(P), \ell_{\rho\sigma}(Q)] = 0\text{,}\)
      🔗
    4. \((\rho,\sigma) \in \operatorname{Dir}(P)\) if and only if \((\rho,\sigma) \in \operatorname{Dir}(Q)\text{,}\)
      🔗
    5. \(\operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(P)\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    To begin with note that \([(1,-1),(0,1)]\) is contained in a half circle, and so, by Remark 1.6.7
    \begin{equation*} (1,-1) < (\rho,\sigma) \Leftrightarrow (1,-1) \times (\rho,\sigma) > 0 \Leftrightarrow \rho + \sigma > 0, \end{equation*}
    and
    \begin{equation*} (\rho,\sigma) \le (0,1) \Leftrightarrow (\rho,\sigma) \times (0,1) \ge 0 \Leftrightarrow \rho \ge 0. \end{equation*}
    We next prove items 1--5 separately.
    🔗
    1. By equality (1.7.2), we have
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = v_{\rho\sigma}(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(Q)) = v_{\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{n}{m} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(P)\right) = \frac{n}{m} v_{\rho\sigma}(P). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    2. Assume by contradiction that this is false. Then, by item 1, we have \(v_{\rho\sigma}(P) \le 0\) and \(v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) \le 0\text{.}\) Hence, by Remark 1.7.3, necessarily \((\rho,\sigma) \ne (1,-1)\text{.}\) Consequently \(\rho + \sigma > 0\text{,}\) and so, by ()
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}([P,Q]) \le v_{\rho\sigma}(P) + v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) - (\rho + \sigma) \le -(\rho + \sigma) < 0, \end{equation*}
    which is impossible, since \(v_{\rho\sigma}([P,Q]) = 0\text{.}\) This concludes the proof of item 2.
    🔗
    3. By Proposition 1.4.12, we must prove that
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}(P) + v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) - (\rho + \sigma) > 0. \end{equation*}
    We first assume that \(\sigma \ge 0\text{,}\) and we consider the three cases:
    1. a) \(\rho = \sigma = 1\text{,}\)
      🔗
    2. b) \(\rho > \sigma\text{,}\)
      🔗
    3. c) \(\rho < \sigma\text{.}\)
      🔗
    In the case a), from Proposition 1.3.10 we obtain directly
    \begin{equation*} v_{11}(P) + v_{11}(Q) - (1+1) = \deg(P) + \deg(Q) - 2 > 0, \end{equation*}
    as desired. In the case b), we note that, again by Proposition 1.3.10, we know that \(P \notin K[y]\text{,}\) and so there is a \((i,j) \in \operatorname{Supp}(P)\) with \(i \ge 1\text{.}\) But then, since \(j\sigma \ge 0\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}(P) \ge v_{\rho\sigma}(i,j) = i\rho + j\sigma \ge i\rho \ge \rho, \end{equation*}
    and by the same argument \(v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) \ge \rho\text{.}\) Hence,
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}(P) + v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) - (\rho + \sigma) \ge \rho - \sigma > 0, \end{equation*}
    as desired. The case c) is similar (use that \(\rho \ge 0\)).
    🔗
    Assume now that \(\sigma < 0\text{.}\) We first note that there exists \((i,j) \in \operatorname{Supp}(P) \cup \operatorname{Supp}(Q)\) with \(i > j\text{.}\) In fact, otherwise \(v_{1,-1}(P) \le 0\) and \(v_{1,-1}(Q) \le 0\text{,}\) which is impossible by Remark 1.7.3. If \((i,j) \in \operatorname{Supp}(P)\text{,}\) then by Remark 1.7.4, we have
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}(P) \ge v_{\rho\sigma}(i,j) > \rho + \sigma. \end{equation*}
    Combining this with item 2, we obtain that
    \begin{equation*} v_{\rho\sigma}(P) + v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) - (\rho + \sigma) > v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) > 0, \end{equation*}
    as desired. In the case \((i,j) \in \operatorname{Supp}(Q)\text{,}\) the same argument works.
    🔗
    4. and 5. By items 2 and 3, and by Proposition 1.5.1(2b), there exist coprime \(n_1, m_1 \in \mathbb{N}\text{,}\) and a \((\rho,\sigma)\)-homogeneous polynomial \(R\text{,}\) such that
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{\rho\sigma}(P) \sim R^{m_1} \quad\text{and}\quad \ell_{\rho\sigma}(Q) \sim R^{n_1}. \end{equation*}
    Clearly \(\ell_{\rho\sigma}(P)\) is a monomial if and only if \(R\) is a monomial, and this happens if and only if \(\ell_{\rho\sigma}(Q)\) is a monomial, which proves item 4. Now, by Remark 1.4.14 we have
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(P) = m_1 \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(R) \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = n_1 \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(R). \end{equation*}
    Consequently, \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \frac{n_1}{m_1} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(P)\text{,}\) and so, \(m_1 = m\) and \(n_1 = n\text{,}\) by (1.7.2). Finally, the same remark yields
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(P) = m \operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(R) \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = n \operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(R), \end{equation*}
    which finishes the proof of item 5.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 1.7.8.

    If \((P,Q)\) is a counterexample to the plane Jacobian conjecture, then there exist coprime \(m,n \in \mathbb{N}\) such that, for all \((\rho,\sigma) \in [(1,-1),(0,1)]\text{,}\) we have
    🔗
    1. \(v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} v_{\rho\sigma}(P)\text{,}\)
      🔗
    2. \(v_{\rho\sigma}(P) > 0\) and \(v_{\rho\sigma}(Q) > 0\text{,}\)
      🔗
    3. \([\ell_{\rho\sigma}(P), \ell_{\rho\sigma}(Q)] = 0\text{,}\)
      🔗
    4. \(\operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(P)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(P)\text{,}\)
      🔗
    5. \(\operatorname{Dir}(P) \cap [(1,-1),(0,1)] = \operatorname{Dir}(Q) \cap [(1,-1),(0,1)]\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Set \(\mathcal{D} := \bigl(\operatorname{Dir}(P) \cup \operatorname{Dir}(Q)\bigr) \cap [(1,-1),(0,1)[\) and write
    \begin{equation*} \mathcal{D} \cup \{(0,1)\} = \{(\rho_0,\sigma_0), \dots, (\rho_k,\sigma_k)\} \quad\text{with } (\rho_j,\sigma_j) < (\rho_{j+1},\sigma_{j+1}), \text{ for all } j = 0,\dots,k-1. \end{equation*}
    We first consider the case \((\rho,\sigma) = (\rho_k,\sigma_k) = (0,1)\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.3.10, we have \(P,Q \in K[x,y]\setminus K[x]\text{,}\) which implies \(v_{01}(P) \ge 1\) and \(v_{01}(Q) \ge 1\text{.}\) Hence,
    \begin{equation*} v_{01}(P) + v_{01}(Q) - (0+1) \ge 1 > 0 = v_{01}([P,Q]), \end{equation*}
    and so, by Proposition 1.4.12, we know that \([\ell_{01}(P), \ell_{01}(Q)] = 0\text{.}\) Thus items 2 and 3 are satisfied, and so, by Proposition 1.5.1(2b), there exist coprime \(n,m \in \mathbb{N}\text{,}\) and a \((0,1)\)-homogeneous polynomial \(R\) such that
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{01}(P) \sim R^m \quad\text{and}\quad \ell_{01}(Q) \sim R^n. \end{equation*}
    Then, by Remark 1.4.14, we have
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{stn}_{01}(P) = m \operatorname{stn}_{01}(R) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \operatorname{stn}_{01}(Q) = n \operatorname{stn}_{01}(R), \end{equation*}
    which yields \(\operatorname{stn}_{01}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{stn}_{01}(P)\text{.}\) Similarly \(\operatorname{en}_{01}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{en}_{01}(P)\text{,}\) which finishes the proof of item 4, for \((\rho,\sigma) = (0,1)\text{.}\) Moreover, we also have
    \begin{equation*} v_{01}(P) = v_{01}(\operatorname{stn}_{01}(P)) = v_{01}\left(\frac{m}{n} \operatorname{stn}_{01}(Q)\right) = \frac{m}{n} v_{01}(Q), \end{equation*}
    which proves item 1 in this case. Assume inductively that \(0 \le j < k\) and that items 1--4 hold for the direction \((\rho_{j+1},\sigma_{j+1})\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{en}_{\rho_j,\sigma_j}(Q) = \operatorname{stn}_{\rho_{j+1},\sigma_{j+1}}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{stn}_{\rho_{j+1},\sigma_{j+1}}(P) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{en}_{\rho_j,\sigma_j}(P), \end{equation*}
    and Lemma 1.7.7 yields items 1--4, for \((\rho_j,\sigma_j)\text{.}\) Hence items 1--4 hold for all \((\rho_i,\sigma_i)\text{.}\) Moreover, item 4 of Lemma 1.7.7 guarantees that
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Dir}(P) \cap [(1,-1),(0,1)] = \operatorname{Dir}(Q) \cap [(1,-1),(0,1)], \end{equation*}
    proving item 5. Finally take \((\rho,\sigma) \in [(1,-1),(0,1)[\text{,}\) and notice that for some \(j\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(Q) = \operatorname{stn}_{\rho_j,\sigma_j}(Q) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{stn}_{\rho_j,\sigma_j}(P) = \frac{n}{m} \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(P), \end{equation*}
    and, again by Lemma 1.7.7, we obtain items 1--4, for \((\rho,\sigma)\text{,}\) concluding the proof.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 1.7.10.

    Let \((P,Q)\) be a Jacobian pair in \(K[x,y]\text{,}\) let \((\rho,\sigma) \in ](1,0),(0,1)[ \cap \operatorname{Dir}(P)\) and let \(F\) be as in Theorem 1.5.6. Then the following cases arise:
    🔗
    1. If \((\rho,\sigma) = (1,1)\text{,}\) then \(\operatorname{Supp}(F) \subseteq \{(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. If \((1,1) < (\rho,\sigma) < (0,1)\text{,}\) then \((\rho,\sigma) = (1,k)\) for some \(k > 1\text{,}\) \(\operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (k+1,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (1,1)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. If \((1,0) < (\rho,\sigma) < (1,1)\text{,}\) then \((\rho,\sigma) = (k,1)\) for some \(k > 1\text{,}\) \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (0,k+1)\) and \(\operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (1,1)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    Moreover, if \((\rho,\sigma) \ne (1,1)\) and if we write \(F = x^u y^v f(z)\text{,}\) where \(z = x^{-\sigma} y^{\rho}\) and \((u,v) = \operatorname{stn}_{\rho,\sigma}(F)\text{,}\) then \(f(z)\) has exactly one linear factor (which is different from \(z\text{,}\) since \(f(0) \ne 0\text{,}\) by Remark 1.4.18).
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    Note that \((1,0) < (\rho,\sigma) < (0,1)\) if and only if \(\rho,\sigma > 0\text{,}\) and \((\rho,\sigma) \in \operatorname{Dir}(P)\) if and only if \(\ell_{\rho\sigma}(P)\) is not a monomial. By Proposition 1.5.5(3) the \((\rho,\sigma)\)-homogeneous polynomial \(F\) is not a monomial, and will find the line \(L\) containing \(\operatorname{Supp}(F)\text{.}\) Since \(v_{\rho\sigma}(F) = \rho + \sigma\text{,}\) we know that \(L\) is the straight line going through \((1,1)\) with slope \(a = -\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\) (see Figure 1.7.11). If \((\rho,\sigma) = (1,1)\text{,}\) then clearly
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Supp}(F) \subseteq \{(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\}, \end{equation*}
    proving item 1. If \((1,1) < (\rho,\sigma) < (0,1)\text{,}\) then the slope \(a\) is in \(]-1,0[\) and \(L\) intersects \(\mathbb{N}_0 \times \mathbb{N}_0\) in more than one point only if \((\rho,\sigma) = (1,k)\text{,}\) for some \(k \in \mathbb{N}\text{.}\) In this case
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Supp}(F) = \{(1,1), (k+1,0)\}, \end{equation*}
    and then \(f(z)\) has degree \(1\text{,}\) \(\operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (k+1,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (1,1)\text{.}\) Finally, if \((1,0) < (\rho,\sigma) < (1,1)\text{,}\) then the slope \(a\) is in \(]-\infty,-1[\) and \(L\) intersects \(\mathbb{N}_0 \times \mathbb{N}_0\) in more than one point only if \((\rho,\sigma) = (k,1)\text{,}\) for some \(k \in \mathbb{N}\text{.}\) In this case
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Supp}(F) = \{(1,1), (0,k+1)\}, \end{equation*}
    and then \(f(z)\) has degree \(1\text{,}\) \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (0,k+1)\) and \(\operatorname{stn}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (1,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Remark 1.7.12.

    Assume that \((P,Q)\) is a Jacobian pair and let \(F\) be the \((1,1)\)-homogeneous polynomial given in Theorem 1.5.6. Write
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{11}(P) = x^i y^j p(z) \quad\text{and}\quad F = x^u y^v f(z), \end{equation*}
    where \(z := x^{-1} y\) and \(p,f \in K[z]\) are polynomials such that \(p(0) \ne 0\) and \(f(0) \ne 0\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.7.10(1), \(\operatorname{Supp}(F) \subseteq \{(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\}\text{,}\) and so by Proposition 1.5.5, the polynomial \(p(z)\) has at most two distinct linear factors.
    🔗
    1. If \(p(z)\) has zero linear factors, i.e., if \(p(z)\) is a constant, then \(\ell_{11}(P)\) is a monomial with support equal to \(\{(i,j)\}\text{,}\) and we have the following three possibilities, which are illustrated in Figure 1.7.13:
      🔗
      • a) \(i \ge 1\) and \(j \ge 1\text{,}\)
        🔗
      • b) \(i = 0\) and \(j > 0\text{,}\)
        🔗
      • c) \(i > 0\) and \(j = 0\text{.}\)
        🔗
      🔗
    2. If \(p(z)\) is a power of a linear factor, then \(f\) has at least one linear factor, and so \((0,2) \in \operatorname{Supp}(F)\) or \((2,0) \in \operatorname{Supp}(F)\text{.}\) Since, by Remark 1.5.7, we know that \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim \operatorname{en}_{11}(F)\) if \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(F) \ne (1,1)\) and \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim \operatorname{stn}_{11}(F)\) if \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(F) \ne (1,1)\text{,}\) we have three possibilities, which are illustrated in Figure 1.7.14:
      🔗
      • a) \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim (0,2)\text{,}\)
        🔗
      • b) \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \nsim (0,2)\text{,}\)
        🔗
      • c) \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \nsim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim (0,2)\text{.}\)
        🔗
      🔗
    3. If \(p(z)\) has two different linear factors, then \(f\) has also two different linear factors, and hence \((0,2),(2,0) \in \operatorname{Supp}(F)\text{.}\) Again by Remark 1.5.7, necessarily \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim (0,2)\text{.}\) This is also illustrated in Figure 1.7.14.
      🔗

    Proposition 1.7.15.

    Assume the Jacobian conjecture is false and let \((P,Q)\) be a counterexample to it. Assume that \(v_{11}(P)\) and \(v_{11}(Q)\) do not divide each other and that we are in one of the cases 1b), 1c) or 2a) of Remark 1.7.12. Then there exists an automorphism \(\varphi\) of \(K[x,y]\) such that
    \begin{equation} \frac{v_{11}(\varphi(P))}{v_{11}(\varphi(Q))} = \frac{v_{11}(P)}{v_{11}(Q)} \quad\text{and}\quad v_{11}(\varphi(P)) < v_{11}(P).\tag{1.7.3} \end{equation}
    Consequently, \(\gcd(\deg(\varphi(P)), \deg(\varphi(Q))) < \gcd(\deg(P), \deg(Q))\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    If we are in the case 2a), then \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim (2,0)\) and so, by (1.4.3) or (1), we are in the case c) of Remark 1.6.12 and we can erase the edge \(\ell_{11}(P)\text{.}\) By this and Example 1.4.26, we arrive at case 1b) without changing \(v_{11}(P)\) nor \(v_{11}(Q)\text{.}\) Now, if we are in the case 1b), then we can apply the automorphism which exchanges \(x\) and \(y\text{,}\) and we arrive at case 1c) without changing \(v_{11}(P)\) nor \(v_{11}(Q)\text{.}\) Thus, we can assume that we are in the case 1c). Then, by Proposition 1.3.10,
    \begin{equation*} (\rho,\sigma) := \operatorname{Succ}_P(1,1) \in ](1,1),(0,1)[. \end{equation*}
    So, by Proposition 1.7.10(2), we have \((\rho,\sigma) = (1,k)\text{,}\) for some \(k \ge 2\text{.}\) Moreover, by Proposition 1.7.8(2), we know that \(v_{1k}(P) > 0\text{.}\) Let \(F\) be as in Theorem 1.5.6 and write
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{1k}(P) = x^r y^s p(z) \quad\text{and}\quad F = x^u y^v f(z), \end{equation*}
    where \(z := x^{-k} y\) and \(p,f \in K[z]\) are polynomials such that \(p(0) \ne 0\) and \(f(0) \ne 0\text{.}\) Again by Proposition 1.7.10, we also know that \(f(z) \sim (z - \lambda)\text{,}\) for some \(\lambda \in K^{\times}\text{,}\) and so, using Proposition 1.5.5, we obtain that \(p(z) \sim (z - \lambda)^t\text{,}\) for some \(t > 0\text{.}\) Moreover, since \((r,s) = \operatorname{stn}_{1k}(P) = \operatorname{en}_{11}(P)\) and we are in the case 1c), we know that \(s = 0\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation} v_{11}(P) = v_{11}(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P)) = v_{11}(r,0) = r \quad\text{and}\quad \ell_{1k}(P) = \mu x^r (z - \lambda)^t = \mu x^{r - tk} (y - \lambda x^k)^t.\tag{1.7.4} \end{equation}
    We define \(\varphi\) by setting \(\varphi(x) := x\) and \(\varphi(y) := y + \lambda x^k\text{,}\) and set \(P_1 := \varphi(P)\) and \(Q_1 := \varphi(Q)\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.6.8
    \begin{equation} \ell_{1k}(P_1) = \varphi\bigl(\ell_{1k}(P)\bigr) = \mu x^{r - tk} y^t \qquad\text{and}\qquad v_{1k}(P_1) = v_{1k}(P).\tag{1.7.5} \end{equation}
    Hence, for each \((i,j) \in \operatorname{Supp}(P_1)\) we have
    \begin{equation*} v_{1k}(i,j) = i + jk \le v_{1k}(P_1) = v_{1k}(P) = r, \end{equation*}
    which, combined with the fact that \(k \ge 1\text{,}\) gives
    \begin{equation*} v_{11}(i,j) = i + j \le i + jk \le r = v_{11}(P). \end{equation*}
    Since \(k > 1\text{,}\) the equality is only possible if \(j = 0\) and \(i = r\text{.}\) But it is impossible that \((r,0) \in \operatorname{Supp}(P_1)\text{.}\) In fact, since \(v_{1k}(r,0) = r = v_{1k}(P_1)\text{,}\) if \((r,0) \in \operatorname{Supp}(P_1)\text{,}\) then \((r,0) \in \operatorname{Supp}(\ell_{1k}(P_1))\text{,}\) which is false because \(t > 0\text{.}\) Consequently, \(v_{11}(P_1) < v_{11}(P)\text{,}\) which is the second condition in (1.7.3). The first condition follows from the series of equalities
    \begin{equation*} \frac{v_{11}(P_1)}{v_{11}(Q_1)} = \frac{v_{1k}(P_1)}{v_{1k}(Q_1)} = \frac{v_{1k}(P)}{v_{1k}(Q)} = \frac{v_{11}(P)}{v_{11}(Q)}, \end{equation*}
    where the first and the third one are true by Proposition 1.7.8(1); and the second one, by Proposition 1.6.8.
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 1.7.16.

    We will say that \(P \in K[x,y]\) is subrectangular if there exists \(a,b \in \mathbb{N}\) such that
    \begin{equation*} (a,b) \in \operatorname{Supp}(P) \subseteq \{(i,j) : 0 \le i \le a \text{ and } 0 \le j \le b\}. \end{equation*}
    In this case we also will say that \(\operatorname{Supp}(P)\) is subrectangular . Finally, we will say that a pair \((P,Q)\) is subrectangular if \(P\) and \(Q\) are.
    🔗
    🔗
    Note that \(P \in K[x,y]\) is subrectangular if and only if \(P \notin K[x] \cup K[y]\text{,}\) and either \(P\) is a monomial or \(\operatorname{Succ}_P(1,0) \ge (0,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Definition 1.7.17.

    A counterexample \((P,Q)\) to the Jacobian conjecture is called a standard counterexample if \(\operatorname{Succ}_P(1,0) > (0,1)\) and there exists \(m,n \in \mathbb{N}\) such that \((P,Q)\) is a standard \((m,n)\)-pair.
    🔗
    🔗

    Remark 1.7.18.

    The pair \((m,n)\) in the previous definition is unique and coincides with the pair \((m,n)\) in Proposition 1.7.8.

    Proposition 1.7.19.

    For each counterexample \((P,Q)\) such that \(v_{11}(P)\) and \(v_{11}(Q)\) do not divide each other, there exists an automorphism \(\varphi\) of \(K[x,y]\) such that \((\varphi(P),\varphi(Q))\) is a subrectangular counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture, which is a standard \((m,n)\)-pair, for some \(m,n \in \mathbb{N}\text{.}\) Moreover
    \begin{equation} \frac{v_{11}(\varphi(P))}{v_{11}(\varphi(Q))} = \frac{v_{11}(P)}{v_{11}(Q)} \quad\text{and}\quad v_{11}(\varphi(P)) \le v_{11}(P).\tag{1.7.6} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By Proposition 1.7.15, applying an automorphism of \(K[x,y]\) if necessary, we can assume that we are not in any of the cases 1b), 1c) or 2a) of Remark 1.7.12. Now, we will modify \(P\) and \(Q\) without changing their degrees, either by using Proposition 1.6.8 or by applying \(\varphi_{y,x}\text{.}\) Hence condition (1.7.6) will be satisfied. If we are in case 3), then
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{11}(P) = x^n (z - \lambda_1)^{n_1} (z - \lambda_2)^{n_2} \quad \text{with } z := x^{-1} y,\; \lambda_1,\lambda_2 \in K^{\times},\; \lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2,\; n_1,n_2 > 0 \text{ and } n_1 + n_2 = n, \end{equation*}
    where for the last equality we have used (2). Applying the automorphism \(\varphi\) of \(K[x,y]\text{,}\) defined by \(\varphi(x) := x\) and \(\varphi(y) := y + \lambda_1 x\text{,}\) we arrive at case 2c). If we are in case 2c), then we apply the automorphism which exchanges \(x\) and \(y\text{,}\) and we arrive at case 2b). In case 2b) we can erase the edge \(\ell_{11}(P)\) (see Remark 1.6.12 c)) and arrive at case 1a). Thus, we can and will assume that we are in case 1a), and so we have
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{11}(P) \sim x^{ma} y^{mb} \quad\text{and}\quad \ell_{11}(Q) \sim x^{na} y^{nb} \quad \text{with } a,b,n,m \in \mathbb{N} \text{ and } m,n \text{ coprime}, \end{equation*}
    where we have used Proposition 1.7.8(5). If necessary, we apply \(\varphi_{y,x}\) to ensure that \(a \le b\text{.}\) Since Theorem 1.5.6(4) guarantees that \(a \ne b\text{,}\) we conclude that \(a < b\text{.}\) We claim that
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{Pred}_{11}(P) \le (1,0) \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{Succ}_{11}(P) \ge (0,1). \end{equation*}
    In fact, to justify the first inequality, suppose for contradiction that \((\rho,\sigma) = \operatorname{Pred}_{11}(P) \in ](1,0),(1,1)[\) and let \(F\) be as in Theorem 1.5.6. By Proposition 1.7.10(2) we know that \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(F) = (0,k+1)\) with \(k > 1\text{.}\) Consequently \(\operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(F) \ne (1,1)\) and thus, by Remark 1.5.7, we obtain
    \begin{equation*} (0,k+1) = \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(F) \sim \operatorname{en}_{\rho\sigma}(P) = \operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) = (ma,mb). \end{equation*}
    This leads to a contradiction, and thus we conclude that \(\operatorname{Pred}_{11}(P) \le (1,0)\text{,}\) as claimed. Similarly one shows that \(\operatorname{Succ}_{11}(P) \ge (0,1)\text{.}\) Consequently
    \begin{equation*} (ma,mb) = \operatorname{stn}_{01}(P) = \operatorname{en}_{10}(P), \end{equation*}
    which implies that both \(P\) and \(Q\) are subrectangular. Hence,
    \begin{equation*} [P,Q] \in K^{\times},\quad \frac{v_{10}(P)}{v_{10}(Q)} = \frac{v_{11}(P)}{v_{11}(Q)} = \frac{m}{n} \quad\text{and}\quad v_{1,-1}(\operatorname{en}_{10}(P)) < 0, \end{equation*}
    where the last inequality follows from the fact that \(a < b\text{.}\) Moreover, since \(v_{11}(P)\) and \(v_{11}(Q)\) do not divide each other, it follows that \(m,n > 1\text{,}\) and therefore \((P,Q)\) is an \((m,n)\)-pair. Let \(F\) be as in Theorem 1.5.6 corresponding to the direction \((1,0)\text{.}\) Since \(v_{10}(F) = 1\text{,}\) we may write \(F \sim x f(y)\text{,}\) where \(f\) is a monic polynomial. Moreover, following Remark 1.6.12, we write
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{10}(P) \sim x^{ma} y^{m_0} \prod_{i=1}^r (y - \lambda_i)^{m_i}, \end{equation*}
    where \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r \in K^{\times}\) are pairwise distinct, \(m_0 \in \mathbb{N}_0\text{,}\) \(m_1,\dots,m_r \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(\sum_{i=0}^r m_i = mb\text{.}\) If \(m_0 = mb\text{,}\) then \(\ell_{10}(P)\) is a monomial. In this case we have \(v_{1,-1}(\operatorname{stn}_{10}(P)) = v_{1,-1}(\operatorname{en}_{10}(P)) < 0\text{,}\) and therefore \((P,Q)\) is a standard \((m,n)\)-pair. If \(r = 1\) and \(m_1 = mb\text{,}\) then, as noted in Remark 1.6.12(c), the edge \(\ell_{10}(P)\) can be erased. In this case, \(\ell_{10}(P)\) becomes a monomial, which implies that \(v_{1,-1}(\operatorname{stn}_{10}(P)) < 0\text{,}\) and therefore \((P,Q)\) becomes a standard \((m,n)\)-pair. Consequently, we can assume that \(r > 1\text{,}\) or \(r = 1\) and \(m_0 > 0\text{.}\) We claim that
    \begin{equation} r \le \begin{cases} c & \text{if } m_0 = 0,\\ c-1 & \text{if } m_0 > 0, \end{cases}\tag{1.7.7} \end{equation}
    where \(c := \deg f\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.5.5(2), to prove this bound it suffices to show that if \(m_0 > 0\text{,}\) then \(f(0) = 0\text{.}\) Assume for contradiction, that \(m_0 > 0\) and \(f(0) \ne 0\text{.}\) Then \(\operatorname{stn}_{10}(F) = (1,0) \ne (1,1)\text{,}\) and by Remark 1.5.7, we would have
    \begin{equation*} (1,0) = \operatorname{stn}_{10}(F) \sim \operatorname{stn}_{10}(P) = (ma,m_0), \end{equation*}
    which leads to a contradiction. Now observe that the bound in (1.7.7) implies \(c > 1\text{.}\) Consequently, \(\operatorname{en}_{10}(F) = (1,c) \ne (1,1)\text{.}\) Therefore, by Remark 1.5.7, we have
    \begin{equation*} (ma,mb) = \operatorname{en}_{10}(P) \sim \operatorname{en}_{10}(F) = (1,c), \end{equation*}
    and thus \(b = ac\text{.}\) But then for some index \(j\) we have \(m_j \ge ma\text{;}\) otherwise, by (1.7.7) we would derive the contradiction
    \begin{equation*} mb = \sum_{i=0}^r m_i < c ma = mb. \end{equation*}
    Let \(\varphi\) be the automorphism of \(K[x,y]\) defined by \(\varphi(x) := x\) and \(\varphi(y) := y - \lambda_j\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.6.8, we have
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{10}(\varphi(P)) = \varphi(\ell_{10}(P)) \sim x^{ma} y^{m_j} \prod_{i \ne j} (y - \lambda_i + \lambda_j)^{m_i}, \end{equation*}
    and therefore, by (1.4.3), it follows that \(\operatorname{stn}_{10}(\varphi(P)) = (ma, m_j)\text{.}\) Now observe that \(m_j = ma\) is impossible; otherwise we would have \(\operatorname{stn}_{10}(\varphi(P)) = (ma,ma) \sim (1,1)\text{,}\) contradicting Theorem 1.5.6(4). Hence,
    \begin{equation*} v_{1,-1}(\operatorname{stn}_{10}(\varphi(P))) = ma - m_j < 0, \end{equation*}
    which shows that \((\varphi(P),\varphi(Q))\) is a standard \((m,n)\)-pair.
    🔗

    Proposition 1.7.21.

    For each counterexample \((P,Q)\) such that \(v_{11}(P)\) and \(v_{11}(Q)\) do not divide each other, there exists an automorphism \(\phi\) of \(K[x,y]\) such that \((\phi(P),\phi(Q))\) is a standard counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture,
    \begin{equation} \frac{v_{11}(\phi(P))}{v_{11}(\phi(Q))} = \frac{v_{11}(P)}{v_{11}(Q)} \quad\text{and}\quad v_{11}(\phi(P)) \le v_{11}(P).\tag{1.7.8} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Proof.
    By Proposition 1.7.19, applying an automorphism of \(K[x,y]\) if necessary, we can assume that \((P,Q)\) is a subrectangular counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture, which is a standard \((m,n)\)-pair, for some \(m,n \in \mathbb{N}\text{.}\) Write \((ma,mb) := \operatorname{stn}_{01}(P)\text{.}\) If \(\operatorname{Succ}_P(1,0) > (0,1)\text{,}\) then there is nothing to prove. Suppose instead that \(\operatorname{Succ}_P(1,0) = (0,1)\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.7.8(2), we know that \(v_{01}(P) > 0\text{.}\) Let \(F\) be the \((0,1)\)-homogeneous polynomial provided by Theorem 1.5.6. Since \(v_{01}(F) = 1\text{,}\) it follows that \(\operatorname{stn}_{01}(F) = (u,1)\text{.}\) We assert that \(u = 1\text{.}\) Indeed, if \(u \ne 1\text{,}\) then by Remark 1.5.7, it must be that \((u,1) \sim (ma,mb)\text{,}\) which leads to a contradiction, as \(0 < a < b\) and \(u \in \mathbb{N}_0\text{.}\) Since \((0,1) \in \operatorname{Dir}(P)\text{,}\) Proposition 1.5.5(3) ensures that \(F\) is not a monomial. Hence, by (1.4.3), we obtain \(\operatorname{en}_{01}(F) = (0,1)\) and \(F \sim xy(x^{-1} - \lambda)\) with \(\lambda \in K^{\times}\text{.}\) Consequently, by Proposition 1.5.5(4), we conclude that
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{01}(P) \sim x^{ma} y^{mb} (x^{-1} - \lambda)^{\ell}, \quad \text{for some } \ell \in \mathbb{N}. \end{equation*}
    According to (1.4.3), this implies that \(\operatorname{en}_{01}(P) = (ma - \ell, mb)\text{.}\) On the other hand, applying Remark 1.5.7 once again, we deduce that \((ma - \ell, mb) = \operatorname{en}_{01}(P) \sim (0,1)\text{,}\) and therefore \(\ell = ma\text{.}\) Let \(\phi\) be the automorphism of \(K[x,y]\text{,}\) defined by \(\phi(x) := x + \lambda^{-1}\) and \(\phi(y) := y\text{.}\) By Corollary 1.6.10
    \begin{equation*} \ell_{01}(\phi(P)) = \phi(\ell_{01}(P)) \sim x^{ma} y^{mb}, \quad \ell_{\rho_1,\sigma_1}(\phi(P)) = \ell_{\rho_1,\sigma_1}(P) \quad\text{and}\quad \ell_{\rho_1,\sigma_1}(\phi(Q)) = \ell_{\rho_1,\sigma_1}(Q), \end{equation*}
    for each \((0,-1) < (\rho_1,\sigma_1) < (0,1)\text{.}\) From this it follows that \(\operatorname{Succ}_{\phi(P)}(1,0) > (0,1)\text{,}\) that \((\phi(P),\phi(Q))\) is a standard \((m,n)\)-pair and that (1.7.8) is satisfied.
