Remark 1.7.12.

Assume that \((P,Q)\) is a Jacobian pair and let \(F\) be the \((1,1)\)-homogeneous polynomial given in Theorem 1.5.6. Write
\begin{equation*} \ell_{11}(P) = x^i y^j p(z) \quad\text{and}\quad F = x^u y^v f(z), \end{equation*}
where \(z := x^{-1} y\) and \(p,f \in K[z]\) are polynomials such that \(p(0) \ne 0\) and \(f(0) \ne 0\text{.}\) By Proposition 1.7.10(1), \(\operatorname{Supp}(F) \subseteq \{(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\}\text{,}\) and so by Proposition 1.5.5, the polynomial \(p(z)\) has at most two distinct linear factors.
🔗
  1. If \(p(z)\) has zero linear factors, i.e., if \(p(z)\) is a constant, then \(\ell_{11}(P)\) is a monomial with support equal to \(\{(i,j)\}\text{,}\) and we have the following three possibilities, which are illustrated in Figure 1.7.13:
    🔗
    • a) \(i \ge 1\) and \(j \ge 1\text{,}\)
      🔗
    • b) \(i = 0\) and \(j > 0\text{,}\)
      🔗
    • c) \(i > 0\) and \(j = 0\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗
  2. If \(p(z)\) is a power of a linear factor, then \(f\) has at least one linear factor, and so \((0,2) \in \operatorname{Supp}(F)\) or \((2,0) \in \operatorname{Supp}(F)\text{.}\) Since, by Remark 1.5.7, we know that \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim \operatorname{en}_{11}(F)\) if \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(F) \ne (1,1)\) and \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim \operatorname{stn}_{11}(F)\) if \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(F) \ne (1,1)\text{,}\) we have three possibilities, which are illustrated in Figure 1.7.14:
    🔗
    • a) \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim (0,2)\text{,}\)
      🔗
    • b) \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \nsim (0,2)\text{,}\)
      🔗
    • c) \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \nsim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim (0,2)\text{.}\)
      🔗
    🔗
  3. If \(p(z)\) has two different linear factors, then \(f\) has also two different linear factors, and hence \((0,2),(2,0) \in \operatorname{Supp}(F)\text{.}\) Again by Remark 1.5.7, necessarily \(\operatorname{stn}_{11}(P) \sim (2,0)\) and \(\operatorname{en}_{11}(P) \sim (0,2)\text{.}\) This is also illustrated in Figure 1.7.14.
    🔗
    🔗
